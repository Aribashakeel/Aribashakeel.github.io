About

Applied mathematics at the interface of biology and physiology

I am a PhD researcher in Applied Mathematics at the University of Birmingham, working under the supervision of Dr Meurig Gallagher and Professor David J. Smith .

My research combines mathematical modelling, analytical methods and scientific computation to understand the mechanisms governing physiological systems.

01
Current Research

Mathematical Modelling to Understand the Treatment of Adrenal Insufficiency

My PhD research develops mechanistic mathematical models of cortisol replacement therapy in adrenal insufficiency. The models incorporate physiological mechanisms including cortisol–protein binding, cortisol–cortisone interconversion, tissue distribution and cortisol excretion.

The broader aim is to understand how treatment strategies interact with physiological mechanisms and how mathematical modelling can help explain cortisol dynamics and inter-patient variability.

Previous Research

Fluid Dynamics & Boundary-Layer Flows

My earlier research focused on fluid dynamics, including rotating flows, boundary-layer theory and models incorporating variable fluid properties.

My MSc dissertation investigated rotating flows over a stationary plane surface, followed by research on MHD boundary-layer flow and numerical modelling using MATLAB.
